# Burns Marketing: Bringing App Development to an Agency

**Role:** Interactive Designer, Burns Marketing Communications  
**When:** 2011–2012 
**Scope:** Websites and mobile apps across a wide roster of agency clients

![Baxa’s “Simply Safe” tradeshow app shown on an iPad and iPhone — a polished product experience for a medical-device maker.](https://adrian3.com/imgs/case-studies/images/Burns-Agency/03-baxa-app.png)

## Introduction

Burns Marketing Communications was a full-service marketing agency with a broad client roster — medical device makers, tech companies, and consumer brands. I joined as an Interactive Designer, and beyond the websites I designed and built, my biggest contribution was helping the agency add a brand-new capability: **app development.** Over a year and a half I helped make mobile apps a core competency at a shop that had been built around branding, advertising, and print work.

## 1. The Web Work

### Client websites, hand-built

The day-to-day of the role was designing and developing websites for clients — in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, WordPress, and Bootstrap. The work spanned industries, from medical and enterprise tech to consumer products. A representative example was Plugless Power, a wireless EV-charging company, where the site had to make a futuristic technology feel simple and trustworthy: *”No plugs. No cords. No hassle.”*

![Two Plugless Power website screens — a bright marketing landing page and a “Fleet Pilot Program” page for an electric-vehicle wireless-charging company.](https://adrian3.com/imgs/case-studies/images/Burns-Agency/02-plugless-power.png)

## 2. Establishing App Development

### A new core competency

Smartphones were still new enough that most marketing agencies didn’t build apps. I helped change that at Burns, designing and developing apps for iOS and Android using Phonegap/Cordova. The most complete example was Baxa, a medical-device maker, where the app was one piece of an integrated tradeshow campaign: the “Simply Safe” iPad and iPhone experience (above), the booth, and the signage all designed as a single launch.

![A 3-D layout of the Baxa tradeshow booth — “Simple Has Arrived” signage, product displays, and screens — designed to integrate with the app.](https://adrian3.com/imgs/case-studies/images/Burns-Agency/04-baxa-booth.png)

The same capability served other clients, like a tradeshow “challenge” quiz app for Cognizant designed to draw people to the booth and start conversations.

![The Cognizant tradeshow quiz app running on a tablet — “Take the Cognizant Challenge… Play now.”](https://adrian3.com/imgs/case-studies/images/Burns-Agency/05-cognizant-app.png)

## 3. Shipping Real Apps

### Proof the capability was real

The surest way to prove an agency can build apps is to ship one to the public app stores. In this period I did exactly that, including **Ghost-O-Meter** — an addicting “ghost detector” that went viral, won a 2012 Art Directors Club of Denver award, and single-handedly established app development as a capability at Burns. _(It earned its own case study: [Ghost-O-Meter](Ghost-O-Meter.html).)_

![The Ghost-O-Meter app on an iPhone — a vintage, gauge-style “ghost detector” UI on a grungy background, with “Available on the App Store / Android Market” badges.](https://adrian3.com/imgs/case-studies/images/Burns-Agency/01-ghost-o-meter.png)

## 4. A Wide Range of Clients

### Breadth was the point

An agency role is, by nature, a tour through many industries at once. Alongside the projects above, I contributed logo design, web, mobile, and tradeshow work for clients including HP, Lenovo, Waterpik, Inspirato, Life Technologies, SolidFire, PCS Wireless, and more.

![An HP learning app on an iPad, used by a student in a library — one of many client projects across the agency’s roster.](https://adrian3.com/imgs/case-studies/images/Burns-Agency/06-hp-app.png)

## Conclusion

Burns was where I learned to move fast across industries and to ship real, downloadable products — not just comps. Helping a traditional marketing agency add app development as a core competency, while designing and building websites for a wide range of clients, gave me the range and the engineering-minded confidence I’d carry into every product and UX role that followed.
