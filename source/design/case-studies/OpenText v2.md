This case study describes my work at OpenText from x to y where I designed application observability products that helped devOps professionals identify problems in their IT systems.

I was brought on in XXXX to build an application observability product. The team I joined was from MicroFocus, a recent acquisition of OpenText, known for enterprise software that bridged legacy mainframe systems with cloud environments.

The first initiative I worked on was building an observability tool to validate that Open Telemetry as a viable new technology capable of replacing the legacy systems that powered existing products.

The first version of the application observability app was designed with select design partners who could stress test our product and steer it towards the most effective use cases. My role was Lead Product Designer as part of a product trio (development, product management, and design) where I designed interfaces, validated concepts with customers, built prototypes, and conducted usability sessions. I created documentation around a site reliability engineer (SRE) persona to help OpenText understand the differences between modern SRE’s compared with more traditional developers. 

I designed the main screen of the observability tool to display the golden signals indicating system health: errors, latency, and throughput. The idea was that the first screen should tell the user where potential problems were happening across potentially thousands of apps in their system. 

To keep development teams aligned, I maintained a UX blueprint for the observability product. Each quarter as we planned our upcoming work, the UX blueprint guided teams around the problems we wanted to solve and suggested direction without locking teams into a pre-decided solution. This is important because planning tends to be an exercise of managing many competing priorities and initiatives. As teams committed to work and the `scope of the projects hardened I iterated on the designs to represent what our team was actually going to build.
