# Inside the Lego Camera

## Still crazy after all these years

![img](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:4000/1*1qDsWkKxBW3u_Jq4EqDy4A.jpeg)3 versions of the Lego camera (35mm panorama, medium format, and 35mm) along with a framed photo taken with the 35mm camera

My Lego camera went viral 16 years ago when I posted some low-quality photos in a blog post showing how the medium format pinhole camera worked. If I had known how much attention my weekend project would generate (thanks to [BoingBoing](http://boingboing.net/2005/10/09/howto-build-a-pinhol.html), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2005/10/10/the-pinhole-lego-camera/), Digg, Metafilter, [Make](http://makezine.com/2005/10/15/how-to-make-a-medium-form/) and others) I would have taken more time with [my original post](http://www.foundphotography.com/PhotoThoughts/archives/2005/10/medium_format_p.html) and the [35mm version follow-up](http://www.foundphotography.com/PhotoThoughts/archives/2006/07/updated_35mm_lego_camera_desig.html).

I still get questions about my cameras and occasional requests for better photos that can be included in books like [Cult of Lego](https://www.amazon.com/Cult-LEGO-John-Baichtal/dp/1593273916). Here is one more look inside the Lego camera along with diagrams pointing out parts of the camera that might trip you up if you try to build your own. If you decide to tackle this project, please send me photos of your results. It has been a great pleasure over the years to see how people have put there own twist on my camera design.

## 35mm Lego Camera

### How it works:

Take the camera’s back off, load the film, then close it back up. Lift the shutter to expose the film for a couple seconds. Crank the knob one or two turns to advance the film. When you have exposed the whole roll of film, open the camera in a dark room or closet. Push the film back in the canister and send it off to get developed.

## Medium Format Lego Camera

### How it works:

Medium format films is a little different from 35mm. You should load the camera in the dark since it doesn’t have the metal canister to protect the film from light. When you advance the film there is a window on the back that lets you see the frame number printed on the film’s backing paper. Once you have exposed the entire roll, roll the film back up in a dark room and send it off to get developed. ([More details here.](http://www.foundphotography.com/2006/05/lego-camera-update/))
